Path planning in high-resolution three-dimensional (3D) voxel maps is a computational bottleneck for mobile robots and Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs): A* with a Euclidean heuristic is optimal but expands large portions of the workspace, and the optimal paths it returns graze obstacle surfaces. We present NACHA* (Neural-Adaptive Cost-Heuristic A*), a graph-search planner with two decoupled controls. A heuristic weight w bounds search effort, and a learned obstacle-risk field, produced by a compact multilayer perceptron and precomputed in a single batched pass, reshapes edge costs through an adaptive multiplier λ₀ to keep paths away from obstacles. We prove that the returned path length never exceeds w(1+2λ₀) times the optimum, and a reliability gate disables the neural term whenever the network’s validation accuracy falls below a threshold. On 62 benchmark environments at 20³ and 50³ resolution, compared against A* on an identical implementation, NACHA* (w = 1.2, λ₀ = 0.1) reduces node expansions by a median factor of 25.8 at 50³ with a mean path-length excess of 0.90% (maximum 3.77%). An ablation shows that this speed-up is produced by the heuristic weight, whereas the neural term governs safety: on volumetric obstacles it lowers the share of the path lying within two voxels of an obstacle from 29.9% (weighted A*) to 8.7% without any increase in path length, and with λ₀ = 0.5 raises the minimum clearance from 1.0 to 2.7 voxels for 1.9% longer paths while expanding 4.8× fewer nodes than A*. We also report where the approach fails: a coordinate-only network cannot represent unstructured random maps, which the reliability gate detects. A final experiment compares the network with an exact distance transform. On a clean map the distance transform is faster and gives larger clearance, but the network needs about 46 KB at any resolution instead of a full grid. When 1–5% of the voxels are corrupted, the distance transform turns every spurious voxel into a high-risk region and marks up to 94% of the true free space as risky, whereas the network smooths the noise, keeps this share near 11%, and needs about 2.8 times fewer expansions.
